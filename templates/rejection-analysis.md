# Rejection analysis by keywords — template

Once a month or after 30–50 applications. The result is a spreadsheet and a dated analysis document.

## What to collect
1. Every application in the tracker with its outcome: rejected before a call, rejected after an interview, silence, reached a person, role closed.
2. The full text of each vacancy: the live ATS (Ashby, Greenhouse, Lever, Workable APIs, the Teamtailor page) or saved copies; keep them as JSON.
3. The CV text that went to that vacancy (`pdftotext`), the form answers and the letter.

## How to count
- From each vacancy, list the must-haves, the core duties and the pluses.
- Check each item against the sent CV, separately in experience and in skills: in the vacancy's own word / only in our synonym / absent.
- Is it true of the candidate: Y — yes, P — adjacent, N — no, ? — ask.
- Per application: the main reason, the second factor, gate knockouts (money, location, level 0–2), verbatim coverage of the must-haves.
- Frequent vacancy words absent from every sent CV — a list with the number of vacancies.
- Check: 20–25 random rows and every quote re-checked by a separate agent (false matches: lab/gitlab, edge/knowledge, async/asyncio).

## Sheets
Findings · Applications — reasons · Frequent words · Words by vacancy · What to change · Master CV — edits · Coverage · Method.

## Reading the outcomes
- A template rejection in 0–3 days is almost always a form filter (location, work authorization, visa), not a reading of the CV.
- A rejection after 1–3 weeks — a person or AI ranking read the CV; look at the wording.
- Compare the share that reached a person separately for applications with and without a knockout.

## What to change after
- The master CV: market words where they are true (Y/P), with the source of each edit; edits only after the candidate's yes.
- The gate: new points from real gaps it missed.
- Message rules: what hurt (questions about location, gaps named on «is a plus»).
- Reason columns in the tracker.
