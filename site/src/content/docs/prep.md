Every stage goes through one cycle:

**`/prep` → learn the sheet → a daily `/mock drill` → a full mock → the interview → `/debrief` the same day**

You need a pack first (`/start`): answers are built from your own answer blocks. No pack and the interview is within a week — say so to `/start`: it confirms only the 5–7 stories this stage needs and sends you to `/prep`; the rest of the pack waits until after the interview.

## Step by step
1. **`/prep <company> <stage> <date and time>`.** Claude finds the format and the people (with sources), weighs the topics, builds answers from your blocks, picks a trainer for the format and plans the days. You get one prep document with a **learning sheet** as its own tab.
2. **Day 1, 60–90 minutes.** Read each story anchor once. Close the sheet and tell each story aloud twice. The pitch three times on a timer.
3. **Every next day, 15–25 minutes: `/mock drill`.** Claude asks the sheet's questions in random order and marks each answer: ✓ — the first sentence and all four steps, no prompt, in time; ~ — a pause, too long, or no number; ✗ — not recalled. The misses come back at the end; in the evening, only the ✗ again.
4. **Two days before: a full mock** at the hour of the interview — `/mock manager <company>`, `/mock system-design`, `/mock live-coding`… by voice where you can. From then on, nothing new: only drills.
5. **The interview day.** Rewrite the map by hand from memory — first sentences only — and the two questions for the interviewer. Check the link, the time zone, the sound.
6. **Right after: `/debrief <company> <stage>`** with the transcript or your notes. New facts go into the pack, answers that worked into blocks, misses into «what to say instead», the next step and the follow-up date into the tracker. Lessons go straight into the next stage's prep.

## A take-home task
Say «a take-home from <company>» with the task. Claude finds out the time box, what is evaluated and the rule on AI tools, plans the work inside the time box, keeps everything running and writes the README with you: assumptions, how to run, what was left out and why. You submit; then you prepare the review call the same way as any stage.

## An offer
Say «<company> made an offer» and paste it. Claude reads it whole, compares it with your floor (never said to anyone) and your other processes, and drafts the answer: no decision in the moment, a counter with one number, a review date with metrics if the base is capped. Before signing — the contract against the offer, line by line. Once the contract is signed and its conditions have cleared — withdrawals from other processes, thanks to references, the tracker closed and a retro. Every message is yours to send.

## How to learn
- **Stories, not answers.** Almost any question is a first sentence plus one of 5–7 stories. A story is an anchor in four steps: the answer in one sentence → the situation → what I did → the number and the lesson.
- **Recall, not rereading.** Rereading feels like knowing and does not hold under pressure.
- **Aloud, timed, shuffled.** 60–90 seconds an answer, a case up to 60; the interviewer will not follow your page.
- **Only the misses again.** ✓ two days running — the question leaves the drill.
- **Words are yours, facts are fixed.** You learn anchors, not texts; numbers and dates come from the pack and never change between rounds.
- **One story per interview,** and across a process — a plan of which round gets which stories: interviewers compare notes.

## Without slash commands
In a claude.ai project the same works in plain words: «prepare me for <company>, <stage>, <date>», «drill <company>», «mock with the CTO», «debrief: here is the transcript».
